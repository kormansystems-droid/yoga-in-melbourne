For workplaces

Yoga at work, taught by teachers you can read about first

Most workplace wellness providers send you a competent stranger. We publish the teacher’s story before she walks in, so your people know who is standing at the front of the room.

Yoga in Melbourne is a publication. We interview the city’s teachers and studios, write their stories properly, and keep a map of who teaches what, and where. That map is now open to workplaces. When you book a class with us, you are booking a named teacher with a page you can read, not an anonymous contractor from a roster.

Why workplaces are asking now

Since 1 December 2025, the Occupational Health and Safety (Psychological Health) Regulations have required Victorian employers to identify psychosocial hazards and take reasonable steps to control them. We are not going to pretend a yoga class is a control for workload, job design or management practice. It isn’t, and any provider who tells you otherwise is selling you something that will not survive a conversation with your health and safety team.

What a good class does is different, and worth having on its own terms. It is a visible, well-attended thing your people can point to, it sits alongside the work you are doing on the causes, and it gives a floor a reason to be full on a Thursday. In a year when Melbourne offices are competing with kitchen tables, that last one has turned out to matter more than anything we could claim about stress.

Chair and desk sessions

No mats, no floor space, and nobody changes out of what they wore to work. Designed for open-plan offices, mixed ages and mixed abilities, and for the people who would never sign up for a yoga class.

Programs designed with you, not from a template

Every provider says they tailor. Here is what that actually means with us.

  1. We ask what is going on. A fifteen minute call. Who is in the building and on which days, what the space allows, whether you are dealing with desk-bound fatigue, a team through a hard quarter, or a floor that has never come back. What has been tried before, and why it stopped.
  2. We match the teacher, not just the class. We know these teachers personally, because we have interviewed them. We choose someone whose style suits the room, who already teaches near your office, and whose story your people can read before the first session.
  3. We start small and measure it. A block, not a contract. Attendance and sign-ups tracked from week one, reviewed with you at the end, and continued only if the room is full.

The practical questions

How much space do we need?
For mat classes, roughly two square metres per person, and a room you can make quiet. For chair and desk sessions, no more space than people already occupy.
Do you bring mats?
Yes, if you want them, priced into the quote. Many workplaces prefer to buy a set once and keep them on site.
Are the teachers insured?
Every teacher we place holds current public liability cover, and we provide certificates of currency on request before the first session.
What if a teacher is sick?
We cover it from the roster and tell you who is coming. Not having a substitute is the main reason these programs quietly stop.
Is there a minimum group size?
No. The price is for the session, not per head, so a class of six costs what a class of twenty-five costs.
How much notice do you need?
Two weeks is comfortable for a weekly program. We can usually do a one-off event in a week, sometimes less.
What if we need to cancel a session?
Forty-eight hours notice and we reschedule it at no charge. Inside that, the session is charged, because the teacher has held the time.
Who do we deal with?
One person, for scheduling, invoicing and anything that needs fixing. One invoice a month, on your terms.
