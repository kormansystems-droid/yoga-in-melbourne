## The rung-0 template, and the two decisions in it

**1. No editorial, verified rather than asserted.** The rendered page contains **0** of:
`.essence`, `.thesis`, `.story`, `.listen`, `blockquote`, `audio`, `.portrait`. A teacher who
has only been listed has no approved photo, no quote and no story, so the skeleton has nowhere
to put one. This is what keeps rung 0 inside the firewall: aggregated schedule data is factual
and public; a page with a standfirst and a characterisation is editorial and needs her yes.

**2. Its layout CSS lives in the template, not in `partials/base.css`.** `base.css` is injected
verbatim into every profile, so adding a `.listing-head` rule there would have rewritten all
five existing pages and broken the byte-identical check. A listing needs exactly one rule
base.css has none for — a header with no portrait beside it, because `.hero` is a two-column
grid that would otherwise leave a hole.

**It states its coverage rather than implying completeness:**

> These are Steph's classes at the studios Yoga in Melbourne currently lists. If she teaches
> somewhere we don't cover yet, or anything here is out of date, tell us and we'll put it right.

That is roster-note findings 4 and 5 built into the skeleton, so every future listing inherits
it. It is also the mechanism that turns a listing into the studio-graph question — the gap is
what prompts her to name the studios YiM cannot see.

## Unchanged

**The editorial firewall.** The listing template carries no editorial precisely so that rung 0
needs no approval — and the ask is still owed regardless, because being the publication that
asks even when it doesn't have to is the differentiator, not friction to optimise away.

Aggregated cross-studio schedules remain the moat. Steph's page is a small proof of it: two
studios on two different platforms, one of them dark, rendered as one timetable. Neither
Mindbody nor Momence can show that, and neither studio can show the other's classes.
