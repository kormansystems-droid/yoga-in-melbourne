# Steph Philip, one L — and the rule it produces

**Date:** 17 August 2026 (evening)
**Live on `main` at `b102d8a`**, ~1.4 hours before the 09:00 UTC story build.
**Corrects:** `YIM-steph-phillip-listing-2026-08-17.md`, shipped hours earlier under the wrong
spelling.

---

## The conclusion first

She spells it **Philip**, one L. Her page shipped as `Steph Phillip` because that is how the
(Here) Yoga Malvern feed spells her, and the feed was the only source we had. Mark asked her.

Corrected: teacher key, `name.family`, template filename, page slug, and a 301 from the old URL.
**Both spellings stay in the aliases.**

**The generalisable point, and the reason this note exists:** the roster note already flagged
spelling variants *between feeds* as the main hidden cost of onboarding. This is a second,
sharper variant — **between the feed and the person**. A feed can be internally consistent,
healthy, and wrong. No amount of cross-feed reconciliation would have caught this one; only
asking her did.

> **Rule for the Inner South cohort: the DM asks how she spells her name.**
> It costs nothing — she is being messaged anyway — and it is the only way to get this right
> before a URL is minted and crawled.

---

## The load-bearing decision: keep both spellings

```json
"aliases": ["Steph Philip", "Steph Phillip", "Stephanie Philip", "Stephanie Phillip"]
```

Dropping the two-L spelling would have been the tidy-looking change and a silent breakage.
(Here) Yoga Malvern publishes her with two Ls, that Momence feed is **healthy**, and it runs at
05:00. `merge.py` matches on the raw feed string, so without the alias she becomes an unmatched
stranger and her **seven Malvern classes disappear from her page** on the next pull.

The canonical key is now her spelling; the feed's spelling is an alias. That is the right way
round: the person is the source of truth for her name, the feed is the source of truth for her
classes.

This also closes the open item from the listing note — "do the aliases catch her?" — answered
before the pull could raise it, and answered in the safer direction.

### Why the redirect matters

The old URL was live and crawlable for an afternoon. It also went out in the listing note. A
404 on a teacher's own page is the kind of thing she would see first — the same class of trust
failure as stale schedule data, and avoidable for four lines of config.

Nothing else referenced the old slug: the story captions derive it from the teacher key, so the
frame-5 link sticker followed automatically. That is the build script earning its keep — a
hand-made card would have carried a dead link into tonight's story.
